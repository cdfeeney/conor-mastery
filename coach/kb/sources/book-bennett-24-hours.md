## Claims
- Everyone gets the same 24 hours daily; it cannot be stolen or saved, only spent well or badly. [¶19]
- Time already wasted does not damage tomorrow; each new day starts clean. [¶42]
- Real change needs a braced will and accepted sacrifice; a neat timetable alone will not do it. [¶39]
- There is no magic method of starting: you simply begin. [¶41]
- Enthusiasm tempts you to over-commit; start with a small amount and allow for accidents and your own human nature. [¶45]
- Early failure costs self-confidence, and most people who fail do so by attempting too much. [¶46]
- Treat the hours outside work as your real day, not a dull prologue to the job. [¶52]
- A modest start (about seven and a half hours a week) is enough, and even that forces real sacrifice because habits are hard to change. [¶75]
- Give yourself generous slack (e.g. 9 to 11.30 for a 90-minute task) so slips do not collapse the plan. [¶77]
- Mind control is the first element of a full life; the mind can be directed, not merely endured. [¶79]
- Concentration practice: pick a subject and drag the wandering mind back repeatedly; the only secret is perseverance. [¶83]
- Set aside daily time to reflect on your principles and whether your conduct matches them. [¶95]
- When you study, limit the scope and spend real time thinking about what you take in, not just consuming it. [¶134]

## Protocol candidates
- morning-routine: Wake somewhat earlier (start with a modest shift, not hours) and use the gained time for your own project before the day's obligations. [¶13]
- reset-focus: Pick one subject, hold attention on it, and each time it drifts, return it without scolding; count the returns. [¶83]
- night-routine: Fixed evening sessions (about 90 minutes, three times a week), treated as non-negotiable like a match or rehearsal. [¶67]
- meditation: Daily concentration practice, ideally half an hour, as a warm-up before harder work. [¶90]
- night-routine: Brief evening reflection: did my actions match my principles today? [¶100]
- no-social-media: Replace idle commute or gap time with deliberate focus or reflection. [¶62]

## Evidence notes
- A 1908 essay and opinion, not research; no data supports its claims.
- Dated: a London office clerk's day, train commutes, newspapers, and the claim that most people sleep too much [¶11]. That is anecdotal and conflicts with modern sleep evidence; do not use it for the sleep habit.
