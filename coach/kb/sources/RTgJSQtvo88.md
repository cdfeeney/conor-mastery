## Claims
- Bright light in the first 30-60 minutes after waking helps put the cortisol peak early in the day and starts a timer for sleepiness roughly 16 hours later. [01:01]
- Phone screens and indoor lights are too dim to replace sunlight for this morning signal; if waking before sunrise, use artificial light only to be awake. [04:02]
- Rough outdoor dose: about 5 min on a clear day, about 10 min with cloud cover, 20-30 min when densely overcast; windows and windshields do not count. [05:32]
- Skip sunglasses for morning light viewing; corrective glasses or contacts are fine; never look at anything painfully bright. [03:02]
- 1-3 minutes of cold water raises core temperature and alertness; morning exercise does likewise. [08:36]
- Delaying caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking may give a longer energy arc and less need for afternoon caffeine. [10:37]
- Caffeine after about 4 pm can degrade sleep architecture even if you feel you sleep fine. [11:07]
- Hard afternoon or evening exercise raises body temperature and delays the body clock; early exercise barely shifts it. [17:43]
- Some low-angle sun in the eyes in late afternoon/evening signals that night is coming. [19:44]
- Between 10 pm and 4 am, avoid bright overhead light and dim screens as far as practical. [21:44]
- A short hot bath or sauna (under 20-30 min) in the evening, then cooling off, eases sleep; cool the bedroom by at least 3 degrees. [22:46]
- Keep wake time within about an hour of normal on weekends. [29:27]

## Protocol candidates
- Go outside within an hour of waking for 5-10 min (more if cloudy), no sunglasses. [05:32]
- Wait 90-120 min after waking before first caffeine; no caffeine after 4 pm (or keep it very small). [10:37]
- From about 10 pm, dim lights and screens; use red light for night-time tasks. [33:38]
- Evening: short hot bath/shower, then keep the bedroom cool. [22:46]
- Wake within +/- 1 hour of your usual time even on weekends. [29:27]
