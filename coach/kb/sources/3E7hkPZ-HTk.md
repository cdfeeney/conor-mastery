## Claims
- The speaker has never had a social media account and reports still having friends, news and collaborators. [01:12]
- He argues social media is an entertainment product, not a fundamental technology. [03:13]
- Platforms employ attention engineers who borrow from casino design to make use compulsive. [04:13]
- The market rewards rare, valuable output that comes from deep concentrated work, not easily copied activity. [05:13]
- Spending the day in fragmented attention (quick checks) may reduce your capacity to concentrate. [08:22]
- Curated feeds of friends' lives can leave you feeling inadequate; he links heavy use to background anxiety. [09:23]
- Quitting feels like a detox: the first two weeks are uncomfortable, then it settles. [10:54]
- Unfragmented attention lets you work with intensity, and intensity can be traded for time. [11:26]
## Protocol candidates
- Expect the first two weeks off social media to be the hardest; plan support for them. [10:54]
- Replace feeds with analog leisure (paper, radio, books at night). [11:56]
## Evidence notes
- The research on depression and anxiety is referenced in general terms, without named studies. The permanent concentration loss is his concern, not a demonstrated result. [08:22]
- No medical items.
