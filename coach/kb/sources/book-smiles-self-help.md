## Claims
- Luck mostly favours the people who keep showing up; ordinary qualities like attention, application and perseverance matter more than special talent. [¶195]
- Skill comes from repetition: train the habit of working, repeat again and again, and ease follows the effort. [¶199]
- Buffon kept fixed morning and evening work hours for forty years until diligence became habitual. [¶212]
- Scott's punctuality was a deliberately cultivated habit; he answered letters the same day and sat at his desk by six after rising at five. [¶215]
- Odds and ends of time add up: one hour a day taken from frivolous pursuits can make a person well informed within ten years. [¶251]
- Energy of will is the core of character, and purpose counts for more than raw talent. [¶388]
- Treat a task as non-negotiable and it soon gets done with ease; the habit of application becomes easier like any other habit. [¶390]
- People of moderate ability achieve a lot by giving themselves wholly to one thing at a time. [¶390]
- Refusing a temptation at once, without long deliberation, gives strength for the next refusal; repeated, it becomes a habit. [¶513]
- Hugh Miller quit drink after one night showed him it dulled his reading; one decisive resolution can be a turning point. [¶514]
- Beating a bad habit works best by setting a high standard, watching your own steps, and resisting small present pleasures for a greater later one. [¶515]

## Protocol candidates
- Fix the same start time and place for the day's main work block, and treat it as non-negotiable. [¶212] (reset-focus)
- Do the next small task today instead of postponing; repeat daily until it feels automatic. [¶199] (morning-routine)
- Do one thing at a time during the focus block; phone away. [¶390] (reset-focus)
- When a craving for nicotine hits, say no immediately rather than debating; log each refusal as a win. [¶513] (no-nicotine)
- At night, compare the day's acts against your own rule and note one adjustment. [¶515] (night-routine)

## Evidence notes
- Historical moral writing (1859), not research; anecdotes of famous men, selected for success.
- Smiles emphasises will and character; modern habit research stresses cues and environment, so treat claims as motivational framing, not mechanism.
