## Claims
- British Cycling's "marginal gains" approach: improve many small things by 1% (gear, sleep pillows, hand washing). [01:06]
- Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement; good habits make time an ally. [03:39]
- Habit loop: you must notice, want, do, and enjoy; failing any stage breaks repetition. [04:43]
- In an exercise study, a motivational talk alone did not change behavior, while writing a specific time and place did; reported 9 of 10 exercised vs 1 of 3 for control. [06:13]
- A specific plan raises follow-through roughly two to three times; the gap is clarity, not motivation. [06:44]
- Speaker published on a fixed schedule (Mon/Thu) regardless of mood or quality, even if only three good sentences. [07:14]
- Pre-mortem plus if-then plans (backup day/time) handle predictable obstacles. [08:45]
- A hospital cafeteria study: adding water options led to 25% more water and 11% less soda with no persuasion. [10:16]
- Add steps between you and bad behaviors; remove steps to good ones (apps buried, social media locked out Mon-Fri). [12:50]
- A photography class graded on quantity outperformed the quality-graded group. [14:22]
- Two-minute rule: any habit can be started in under two minutes as an entrance ramp. [16:25]
- Twyla Tharp's habit was hailing the cab to the gym, not the workout itself. [16:56]
- Good habits cost now and reward later; use immediate feedback such as the Seinfeld calendar chain. [18:28]
- "Never miss twice": recover fast after a miss; top performers err but return quickly. [20:00]
- Identity is built by evidence from repeated action. [22:04]
## Protocol candidates
- Required field: day, time, place for each habit. [06:44]
- After a miss, prompt an immediate next-day recovery ("never miss twice"). [20:00]
- Offer a 2-minute version of every habit as the start. [16:25]
- Suggest removing friction to the good habit and adding friction to social media. [12:50]
## Game-design notes
- Streak chain with a "never miss twice" grace rule instead of reset-to-zero shame. [20:00]
