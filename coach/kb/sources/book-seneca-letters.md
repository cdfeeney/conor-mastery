## Claims
- Time is the one thing truly ours, and much of it leaks away through carelessness, idleness or off-purpose activity. [¶3]
- Hold every hour; do today's task so you depend less on tomorrow, because postponing lets life speed by. [¶4]
- Being able to stay put and keep your own company marks a well-ordered mind; restlessness signals a disordered one. [¶8]
- Reading everything shallowly nourishes nothing; stay with a few master thinkers and digest them. "Everywhere means nowhere." [¶8]
- Crowds are the main thing to avoid while you are still recovering; you come back changed, and old agitations return. [¶35]
- Everyone you mingle with tends to make some vice attractive or tint you with it. [¶35]
- Review each day, because people go wrong by only looking forward; plans for the future depend on the past. [¶1061]
- Practise hardship in comfortable times: a few days of coarse food, rough bed and cloak, asking "Is this the condition that I feared?" [¶156]
- Noise that carries no meaning (waves, wind) need not break concentration; words demand attention more than noise. [¶1063, ¶531]
- Rest does not come from silence; real tranquillity comes from a calm mind, and night merely changes the form of worries. [¶534]

## Protocol candidates
- Opening ritual: name today's one task and begin; treat the day as spent, not stored. [¶4] (morning-routine)
- Read one chosen book only; pull one idea to carry through the day. [¶9] (reset-focus)
- Feed and social time: skip crowds and feeds for a week when you feel unsteady; notice what returns. [¶35] (no-social-media)
- Evening: replay the day, noting what you did, what leaked time, and what you would hide; then plan tomorrow from it. [¶1061] (night-routine)
- During a focus block, treat background noise as waves; do not chase it. [¶1063] (reset-focus)
- When breath tightens, keep calm self-talk and wait for it to ease. [¶515] (deep-breathing)

## Evidence notes
- Historical moral writing (c. 65 CE), not research; claims are reflective advice, not evidence.
- The Stoic framing (fortune, death) is philosophical; use only as motivational context.
