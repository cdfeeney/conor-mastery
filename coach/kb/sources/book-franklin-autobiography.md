## Claims
- Merely believing it is in your interest to be good does not stop slips; old habits must be broken and new ones established. [¶194]
- Franklin worked on one virtue at a time rather than all at once, so attention stayed focused. [¶198]
- He ordered the targets so early gains help later ones; temperance came first because it brings a clear head. [¶198]
- He kept a paper grid: a row per virtue, a column per day, marking each fault with a dot. [¶199]
- He gave each virtue one week of strict attention, only logging faults for the others, then added the next. [¶202]
- Weeding one garden bed at a time is his image for not attacking everything at once. [¶202]
- He fixed a daily schedule and bracketed it with two questions: what good shall I do this day, and what good have I done today. [¶213]
- On first trying self-examination he was surprised how many faults he had, but saw them diminish. [¶214]
- He moved the log to a wipeable memorandum book so each new cycle could restart easily. [¶214]
- Order was the virtue he failed at most, since his schedule depended on other people's hours; he still felt the effort improved him. [¶215]
- Falling short of perfection still helped, as copying engraved handwriting improves your own hand. [¶216]
- He credited temperance for his health and industry and frugality for his fortune. [¶217]
- A friend's feedback about his pride led him to add humility. [¶222]

## Protocol candidates
- night-routine: Each evening, mark any slip against the current focus habit on a simple grid; no marks means a clean day. [¶199]
- morning-routine: Start the day by asking "What good shall I do this day?" and set the day's resolution. [¶213]
- night-routine: End the day with the question "What good have I done to-day?" [¶213]
- reset-focus: Spend a whole week on one habit at a time, then add the next after a clean week. [¶202]
- no-nicotine: Log any slip daily on the grid and review the pattern weekly. [¶214]

## Evidence notes
- A personal memoir (written 1771-1788), not research; results are self-reported.
- Dated: religious framing, prayers, and the virtue list (e.g. chastity). Franklin admits he never reached perfection [¶216].
