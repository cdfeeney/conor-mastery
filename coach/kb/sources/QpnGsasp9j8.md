## Claims
- Unaided cold-turkey quitting succeeds only about 5% of the time, and many of those later relapse. [00:02]
- Roughly 75% of quit attempts relapse in the first week, per the review he cites. [05:43]
- Nicotine is reinforcing partly because of the oral/hand habit as well as dopamine. [06:15]
- A Stanford clinical hypnosis session for smoking cessation reported about 23% quitting, versus 5% unaided; he suggests it likely carries over to vaping. [02:34]
- The Reverie app (by the same researcher) includes a smoking-cessation hypnosis program. [03:38]
- A prescription antidepressant (bupropion) lifts quit rates to roughly 20%, but has real risks such as seizures and liver/kidney cautions. [07:47]
- Combining nicotine replacement forms, rotating patch, gum, spray, may work better than one alone; the rotation logic is his reasoning rather than a detailed study. [12:27]
- Waking up already in nicotine withdrawal is why morning urges are strong. [13:59]

## Protocol candidates
- Do not rely on willpower alone; add support such as a hypnosis-based program (e.g. Reverie). [04:09]
- Plan for the first week as the highest-relapse period. [05:43]
- Talk to a doctor about a quit plan, including any medication or nicotine replacement. [08:47]

## Evidence notes
- Quit-rate figures are cited as study results, but he names only a 1993 hypnosis study [02:04] and a 2020 review [05:12]; the hypnosis result comes from one lab.
- Medically sensitive: bupropion (Wellbutrin) and nicotine replacement (patch, gum, nasal spray). He says he is not a psychiatrist. Talk to a doctor; no dosages recorded here.
- The "keep dopamine variable" rationale for rotating replacement forms is speculative. [15:01]
