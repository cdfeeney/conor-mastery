## Claims
- Alcohol sedates the cortex rather than producing natural sleep, so a nightcap is not a real sleep aid. [00:33]
- Alcohol fragments sleep with extra awakenings that drinkers often do not remember, so they miss the cause of feeling unrefreshed. [01:03]
- Alcohol suppresses REM (dream) sleep, which supports emotional and mental health. [01:34]
- Marijuana speeds falling asleep but also blocks REM, and he says it can worsen next-day anxiety and mood. [02:36]
- CBD appears not to affect sleep on limited data; he calls the conclusion premature. [03:08]
- Lost sleep cannot be fully banked or repaid; recovery sleep never returns all hours lost. [04:11]
- Short sleep during the week and binge-sleeping on weekends ("social jetlag") is linked to worse health, such as diabetes, obesity and cardiovascular problems. [05:13]
- Recommendation: consistent 7-9 hours every day, weekdays and weekends alike. [05:44]
- Set a "last orders" alarm about 15 min before the time you should start getting into bed, and do pajamas/teeth beforehand. [06:46]
- Use one morning alarm, not snooze; alarms stress the cardiovascular system and repeating that daily adds up. [07:18]

## Protocol candidates
- Keep the same bed and wake times seven days a week. [06:15]
- Set a wind-down alarm 15 min before bedtime; do hygiene tasks first. [06:46]
- No snooze: one alarm, then get up. [07:48]
- Swap the evening drink for a non-alcohol wind-down. [00:33]

## Evidence notes
- Mostly presented as research (mortality and health-outcome study, sleep-recovery experiments) but no study is named; the five-hour mortality figure [04:43] comes from one study he does not cite. The rat REM-deprivation claim is animal data. [01:34]
- The cardiovascular snooze-button effect is extrapolated from alarm-response data; the long-term harm is his inference. [07:48]
- Medically sensitive: marijuana/CBD use for sleep; talk to a doctor.
