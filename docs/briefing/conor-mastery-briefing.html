2. How gamification actually works

Points and badges alone don't change behaviour. A habit game works when it pulls on a handful of well-understood levers. These are the ones that matter for you:

TriggerSomething prompts you: a notification, the morning gate, the app icon.
ActionThe smallest possible step: tick a habit, start a timer.
RewardSomething you feel right away: grit, XP, a sound, a sprite popping.
InvestmentYou put something in that makes coming back worth more: the garden you built.

The five levers

LeverWhy it worksHow the apps use it
Visible progressSeeing yourself get better (competence) is the strongest motivator there is.Levels, belts, a garden that grows from bare ground.
Variable rewardA reward you can't predict is more compelling than a fixed one. It's the same pull as a slot machine, used for good here.Habitica's 3% critical hits and random drops. Ours has none yet.
Loss aversionLosing something hurts about twice as much as gaining it feels good.Streaks, Habitica's health bar. Too harsh, though, and one bad week makes people quit.
InvestmentThe more you've built, the harder it is to walk away.Monke's island, our garden.
Adaptive difficultyEasy wins should pay little and hard things should pay a lot, or the game gets boring.Habitica pays more for neglected habits. Ours pays the same every time.

The trap to avoid: points can crowd out the real reason you're doing a habit. Researchers call this the overjustification effect. That's why every habit card leads with your identity line ("I'm free. I don't need it.") and the coach's reason, with the reward second.

Habitica's best idea: habits that are weak pay more

Every Habitica task has a hidden "value". Doing it raises the value and missing it lowers it. The reward is 0.9747value, so a neglected habit pays up to about 3.4× and a habit you've mastered pays about 0.6×. The game automatically steers you toward what you're avoiding.

6. Art: from emoji to a pixel dojo

You're right that emoji can't carry this. The plan is a 16×16 top-down pixel-art garden built on Ninja Adventure by pixel-boy. It's a free asset pack released as CC0 (public domain, fine to use in any app), with ninja characters, Japanese village tiles, trees, houses, UI, sound effects and music. Original sprites for the belt-coloured character, dojo, koi pond and torii will match its palette.

Game stateWhat you'll see
Habit strengthEach habit is a plant: wilted, healthy or blooming.
Low energyThe garden desaturates and leaves fall.
Perfect DayLanterns light up at night and fireflies appear.
Level-upYour belt colour changes, with a particle burst.
FocusYour character meditates by an animated fire.
